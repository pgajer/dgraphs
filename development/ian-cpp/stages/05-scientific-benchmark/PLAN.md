## Conditional-mean experiment

This is explicitly **transductive**: all 200 predictor coordinates, including eventual evaluation points, may enter the graph; outcomes may not. No claim about predicting a newly arriving point is made. For each saved coordinate dataset, use ten independently seeded response/split replicates. Randomly assign 50 training, 50 tuning and 100 evaluation points, independently of coordinates and responses. Generate Y=f(X)+epsilon with independent Gaussian noise of standard deviation 0.2. Only training outcomes form predictions; tuning outcomes select a candidate by mean squared prediction error, with first-listed tie breaking. Evaluation outcomes and known means never select tuning parameters. Score final predictions on the 100 evaluation points against f(X), the known conditional mean; also retain observed-outcome error separately. Keep the same splits/noise across methods for paired comparisons.

Compare six declared estimators: training mean; unweighted Euclidean k-nearest labeled neighbors with k in [3,5,10,20,40]; Euclidean Gaussian kernel; Gaussian kernel using shortest distances on the final IAN graph; powers of the returned IAN affinity; and an explicitly labeled oracle Gaussian kernel using true intrinsic distances. Each nonconstant method has exactly five candidates. For each distance kernel, use exp(-d^2/(2*h^2)), with h equal to the median per-point tenth finite neighbor distance in its full geometry times [0.5,1,2,4,8]. If fewer than ten finite neighbors exist, use the farthest available; the global median ignores rows without a neighbor. Report missing geometry rather than invent a scale if no finite distance exists. IAN affinity powers are [0.25,0.5,1,2,4]. For every weighted prediction normalize weights over training points only; if their sum is zero use the training mean and count the fallback. Infinite graph distance has zero weight. The oracle comparator uses privileged geometry and is not a deployable competitor or a guaranteed optimum.

Retain every candidate tuning loss, selected index, prediction, split, noise vector, true mean, observed outcome and fallback count. Report method means over ten replicates within each coordinate dataset, then the mean and observed range across the three independent coordinate datasets per geometry. Report paired method differences at the dataset level. Do not treat dependent vertices or ten shared-graph replicates as independent datasets; no p-values or population confidence intervals are planned. More tuning candidates or different estimators require a new declared study, not retrospective rescue.
