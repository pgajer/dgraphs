mclaren2019 — verified

Metadata
Michael R. McLaren, Amy D. Willis and Benjamin J. Callahan. Consistent and correctable bias in metagenomic sequencing experiments. eLife 8:e46923 (2019). DOI: 10.7554/eLife.46923.
Context and supported claim
Main Implication and Consequences for Graph Construction: observed proportions combine biological composition with taxon-specific efficiencies and normalization; ratios and proportions respond differently to this bias.
Source evidence
Results, “A mathematical model of MGS bias,” equations 1–2 model observed composition as actual composition multiplied by relative efficiencies. The following normalization and ratio discussion explains why composition changes affect proportions. The graph-distance warning follows from applying distances to these distorted observations.

clausen2025 — verified

Metadata
David S. Clausen, Sarah Teichman and Amy D. Willis. Estimating Fold Changes from Partially Observed Outcomes with Applications in Microbial Metagenomics. arXiv:2402.05231v2, 14 March 2025.
Context and supported claim
Main Implication and Practical Rule: sample- and category-specific measurement perturbations limit direct inference about underlying biological quantities without identifying assumptions.
Source evidence
Section 2.2, equations 1–5 and Lemma 1 distinguish unobserved means from observed means with multiplicative sample and category effects, and identify equivalence classes rather than unrestricted absolute effects. Section 1 identifies radEmu as the implementing software.
